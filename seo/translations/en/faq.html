Order status and shipping Most asked

Tracking, delivery times, fees, address changes, shipping outside Canada.

Where is my order?

Two possibilities.

The product was in stock. The order leaves Québec City within a few business days. You receive an email with the tracking number when it ships.

The product was on pre-order. The estimated ship date is shown on the product page and in your confirmation. The order ships after that date, not before.

Small orders sent by stamp (seeds, seed bombs) have no tracking number.

My tracking number isn't moving

Tracking activates when the carrier picks up the parcel, not when the label is created. A “label created” status for a few business days is normal.

If nothing moves after a week, write to us with your order number. We check on our end and follow up with the carrier if needed.

The parcel is marked “delivered” but I haven't received anything

First check the post office listed in the tracking, with a neighbour, and the address on your order confirmation. Most cases are solved right there.

Otherwise, write to us with your order number. We open an investigation with the carrier and find a solution with you.

Sizing and size exchanges

Sizing advice is the first question before buying. Two careful measurements prevent almost every exchange, and if it doesn't fit, exchanges are free in Canada.

How do I choose my mitten size?

Two measurements are enough: the width across the knuckles and the total length of the hand. Where you measure matters, half a centimetre can change the size.

If you fall between two sizes, take the larger one. A slightly roomier mitten keeps your fingers away from the seams and stays warmer.

Contradictory result? Send us a photo of your hand next to a ruler or tape measure and we will confirm the size.

See the size guide with photos
Which size for a coat or vest?

Three measurements: chest, waist and hips, taken with a soft tape measure without pulling tight. Between two sizes, take the larger one. As a last resort, take the size of your usual winter coats.

See the coat and vest guide
Which size for the insulated insoles?

If you wear a half size, take the size up. An unpadded 6 mm band surrounds the insulated part, which leaves room to trim the outline.

I ordered the wrong size

You can exchange it within 15 days of delivery, as long as the product has not been worn or washed. We will ask you to confirm your measurements against the product's size guide.

Exchanges are free in Canada: prepaid return label, no handling fee, and the replacement ships as soon as we receive yours.

Products and care

What milkweed does in a product, what it doesn't do, and how to keep your items for a long time.

Is it really warm? Why milkweed?

Milkweed floss is a hollow fibre: each fibre traps air inside, and that air is what insulates. It is very light, naturally water-repellent, plant-based and free of animal materials.

The warmth of a finished product also depends on its outer fabric, its thickness and how it is used. Our job is to turn the fibre's properties into products that hold up in real life.

Everything about milkweed as insulation
Does the insulation pack down over time?

A little, like any insulation, but it keeps its insulating power. Many insulations rely on the air between the fibres, which disappears under compression. In milkweed, the air is held inside each hollow fibre, and it stays there.

Are the mittens waterproof?

Water-resistant, not 100% waterproof. The outer fabric, a tightly woven polyester, sheds water long enough to shake it off, and the milkweed fibre is itself water-repellent, inside and out. We chose not to use plastic membranes so your hands can breathe.

Seeds, seed bombs and planting

Milkweed is a native perennial: a little preparation the first year, then it takes care of itself.

When and how should I plant?

Seed bombs germinate when the soil stays above 20 °C for several days in a row. If they arrive before the season, keep them in the fridge. If they have already started to sprout, set them on the surface of a pot of soil under clear plastic, near a sunny window.

Allow for a season of patience: milkweed flowers from the second year on.

Read the planting guide
Do the seeds need to be stratified?

Yes. Our seeds are not stratified: plan on about 30 days in the cold before sowing. It delays planting a little, but it gives the best germination rates.

My seed bombs didn't sprout

It happens. Germination depends on the soil type, sunlight, watering and temperature. Write to us and tell us where, when and how you planted: we will advise you, and we reship the seed bombs you ordered, right away or for the next planting season.

Is milkweed invasive?

Vigorous, yes; invasive, no. Common milkweed is a native plant perfectly adapted to our ecosystems, and it spreads through its rhizomes. Nothing like giant hogweed or Japanese knotweed, which are harmful exotic plants.

If you are short on space, butterfly milkweed, swamp milkweed and tall milkweed spread much less.

Which species for my hardiness zone?

Every species we offer in packets or seed bombs suits zones 3 to 9, which covers populated Québec and most of North America.

Is it toxic to children or pets?

Milkweed latex is toxic, but so bitter that a child, dog or cat will never eat enough to get sick. The real risk is for livestock, when dried milkweed ends up in hay and loses its bitter taste. In a garden or a field, the danger is greatly overestimated.

How much area does a seed bomb cover?

A 4-cell stick covers about 4 square feet, or 1 square foot per cell. Give common milkweed more room, since it spreads; butterfly and swamp milkweed can be sown closer together.

Lasclay, milkweed and monarchs

Our mission, our trade-offs, and the questions we get asked about monarchs.

Does harvesting milkweed hurt monarchs?

No, quite the opposite. The caterpillars eat the leaves and the butterflies feed on the flowers, from spring to late summer. The floss we harvest is in the pods, in the fall, when the monarchs have already left for Mexico.

Giving milkweed economic value gives farmers a reason to grow it rather than pull it out. The more of it there is in the fields, the more places monarchs have to breed.

The monarch butterfly and milkweed
Does a purchase save a monarch?

Not one specific butterfly, and we won't claim it does. Each purchase supports a supply chain that makes milkweed profitable to grow, and therefore more present in the landscape. It is a systemic link, not a one-for-one offset.

Where are your products made?

Growing the milkweed, processing it into insulation, designing the products and quality control all happen in Québec, at our workshop in Limoilou. That is the heart of what we do, and it isn't going anywhere.

The textile assembly of most finished products is done by partners outside Québec, mainly in Tunisia. The insulation leaves from here, is assembled there, and comes back inside the product. For the coats, the shell is made in Tunisia; the insulation and the removable inserts are still processed in Limoilou, which makes a coat around $300 possible.

Why? Québec's textile industry no longer has certain skills or the machinery for technical pieces at an accessible price. Doing everything here would have made milkweed unaffordable. A few products are still made entirely here, including the milkweed oil cosmetics: it is stated on their product page.

Why the name “Lasclay”?

They are the first two syllables of “asclépiade”, the French word for milkweed: L'AS-CLÉ. It is pronounced “Lass-clay” in French and in English. Spelling it with a y avoids accents, which are impossible in a domain name, and makes an invented word that Google doesn't autocorrect to “la clé”.
