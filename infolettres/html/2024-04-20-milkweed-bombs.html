Is there any greater pleasure than the return of warm weather, singing birds, and the snow slowly melting down? With the Spring days getting longer, we look forward to summer and start thinking about gardening.
 
About that, our national milkweed planting campaign, now in its fourth year, is well underway. Thanks to our great community, thousands of new monarch butterfly gardens have been planted since 2021 and we are really, really proud of that! This year, we've added three new milkweed species in the seed packet format: Butterfly milkweed, Swamp milkweed and Showy milkweed, all of which are magnificent and have the advantage of spreading a little less than the common variety, for those with less gardening space.

However, we've known for some time that milkweed, as beneficial as it is to biodiversity, is not particularly easy to sprout. Indeed, the seeds of all Canadian native milkweed species, like most northern plants, require a cold, damp treatment of at least 30 days to emerge from dormancy and sprout, a fairly technical process that is a challenge even for experienced gardeners. 

Many people have therefore asked us to help them to make milkweed planting easier by supplying seeds that are already cold-treated and ready to plant. So, last year, we designed and developed a new product to meet all these demands, and it's making a comeback this year in an improved version:

Milkweed seed bombs! 👇
 
 
This innovative product combines enriched potting soil, a natural clay binder and milkweed seeds in several species. Already cold-treated, these seed bombs are ready for planting as soon as you receive them and can be used until late in the summer, making life easier for gardeners. You just have to drop the seed bombs on top of your soil and cover it lightly. Since they're already coated with fertile soil, there's nothing more to do!

Marketed as splittable sticks for their first summer in 2023, we're now offering seed bombs in an improved format this year: little balls that are much stronger (less likely to break in the mail), dry out slower and do a better job of protecting the seeds they contain.
