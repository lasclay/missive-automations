Reception & Understanding the packaging

1. Format
One bomb = One 4" x 3/4" stick. Each bomb is divisible into 4 small squares (cells) of about 1" x 3/4"
 
2. Packaging & identification
In each order, the bombs are placed in plastic bags to preserve their moisture and then identified by species (for milkweed bombs) or botanical theme (for native flower bombs). For example, for an order containing three swamp milkweed bombs, one hummingbird flower bomb and one renaturalization bomb, there would be 3 plastic bags in the shipping envelope: one bag with the 3 swamp milkweed bombs and two other bags each containing one bomb per theme.
 
3. Consequences of transportation
3.1 It's possible that the sticks have already separated during shipping and that you only have 1" x 3/4" cells in the bags. To make sure that you've received the right amount of bombs, just count the number of small cells and divide by 4! It doesn't affect germination in any way, it just means that Canada Post has done the separating work for you 🙂
 
3.2 In rare cases, the seed bombs may have disintegrated completely. This can be caused by two things: either the roads were much bumpier than usual, or the bombs were inadequately mixed on our side, with a poor ratio of potting soil VS clay binder. In this case, it complicates the planting work a little, but you should be able to manage it by distributing the disintegrated mixture equally at the places where you had planned to plant your cells. If it's really too difficult, don't hesitate to contact us and we'll take care of it as quickly as possible.

Planting

Here's a little visual planting guide we've put together that should help a lot. If you still have questions after reading this guide, we've prepared some answers to frequently asked questions, a little further below.

 

Frequently asked questions

1. When should I plant my seed bombs?

It's best not to plant them before June, to minimize the risk of late night frosts. Depending on where you live, the ideal planting period is from June 15 to August 1. In the fall, it's best to plant untreated seeds directly in the ground. Winter will defeat their defenses!

2. Where do I plant my seed bombs?
The answer depends on the type of bombs you have and the seeds they contain. You'll find all the botanical information you need on our website, on the seed bombs product pages: soil type, sunlight, plant type, etc. In the case of milkweed bombs, since each bomb contains a single species, we were able to add basic botanical information directly on the bags, as illustrated below:
 
3. How to improve the yield and success of the seed bombs?

In terms of preparation, we've already done all we can to encourage sprouting. However, a good way to ensure longer-term plant success is to weed out a radius of at least 6 inches, but ideally 12 inches, around where each cell will be planted, to limit competition from other perennials.

4. Is it normal for my seed bombs to have mold on the surface?

Yes, it is! Our seed bomb mix contains mycorrhizal spores, beneficial fungi that germinate and help plant roots gather nutrients deeper in the ground once established.

5. Is it possible to store unused bombs for later?

Yes, by putting them back in the fridge as soon as you receive them. However, there is a risk of greatly reducing future germination by doing that. Explanation: During shipping, the bombs return to room temperature for a few days, which may give the seeds the signal to come out of their cold dormancy and start germinating. It's possible that by putting them back in the fridge, the seeds that hadn't yet germinated will resume their dormancy, but those that had started to germinate will probably die.
