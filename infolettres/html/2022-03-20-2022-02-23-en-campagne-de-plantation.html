The first reason why we founded Lasclay in September 2020 was our genuine passion for milkweed and its mysteries, as well as our desire to improve the dire situation of monarch butterflies

Milkweed has been a mainstay of our ecosystems for thousands of years.

  • It is an abundant source of nectar for many native pollinators.
     
  • It is the exclusive host plant for several insects, beetles and other organisms that have co-evolved with it. These specialized species are entirely dependent on milkweed for food and/or reproduction. The most iconic insect of this group is the monarch butterfly, which is still an endangered species today.
     
  • Milkweed’s unique biological traits make it grow easily even in the least fertile soils, making it a powerful carbon sink. With its prolific biomass production and deep root system, a milkweed colony can store a significant amount of carbon in the soil for a long time. A milkweed plantation is a unique GHG emission control and a valuable defense against climate change 

This mainstay is now under pressure. Milkweed populations have been steadily declining in North America in the last decades.

Like many other native plants, milkweed is gradually disappearing for 3 main reasons:

  • Its systematic elimination from rural environments with herbicides, because it competes too well with commercial crops and is mildly poisonous for livestock.
     
  • Urban sprawl on farmland, meadows and vacant lots.
     
  • Its widespread categorization as a noxious/invasive weed by many towns and villages, which leads to urban milkweed colonies being mowed or sprayed with herbicides.

Despite the best efforts of various environmental organizations (including the David Suzuki Foundation), the monarchs came close to extinction in 2013.

  • This near-extinction event coincides with the beginning of Canada’s milkweed industry in 2013, when hundreds of hectares of milkweed were massively planted, all at once. Since then, monarch populations mysteriously rebounded to their highest level in almost 20 years. Perhaps this is a coincidence...
     
  • But we do not believe it is a coincidence. Logic and science point to the same conclusion. The Canadian milkweed industry has recreated gigantic habitats for monarchs, potentially saving them from extinction.
