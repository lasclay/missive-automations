Our first year has taught us a lot in a very short time. A material as new and beautifully complex as milkweed floss brings many technical challenges to manufacturing, especially to us who insist on using 100% milkweed to insulate our products (which we are the only ones to do). Last year, it became apparent along the way that making quality mittens with 100% milkweed insulation was labor-intensive and threatened our very mission: to democratize milkweed by offering affordable, locally-made products while helping to safeguard monarch populations.

Where others might have given up, we doubled down on sheer effort and creativity by reinvesting all of our profits to automate the most time-consuming steps of the process and keep the manufacturing in Canada. This has allowed us to design a new and much-improved version of our mittens, at almost the same price as last year. Here are some examples of improvements we made:

Mitten design
New pattern

  • Even more comfortable and versatile (for both walking and shoveling!), while keeping a minimalist look.

Mittens clip

  • Last year, we included a removable clip that looked great but was a bit too easy to lose. On the new mittens, we've added loops on the wrist, which allow you to attach the mittens together with a smaller clip, 3d-printed in the image of our logo, and taking 10 times less material and time to make than the old one (pictures in the link below).

Inner lining

  • Instead of regular polyester, we now use recycled polyester for the inner lining of the mitten. The new recycled fabric was the same price and just as comfortable, so it was a no-brainer for us to choose this and reduce our environmental footprint.

Quilting thread

  • Last year, a braided nylon thread was used for the grid-pattern quilting on the inside of the mitts. Nylon has the advantage of being slightly more flexible and soft, but it reduces the recyclability of the product at the end of its lifecycle, as you end up with a combination of polyester, nylon, and milkweed in the lining. Simply choosing a polyester thread reduces the number of materials in the lining to two (polyester and milkweed), making it easier to disassemble and recycle/compost.

Non-slip palm patch

  • By popular demand, we decided to add a non-slip palm to the new mitten. We thought long and hard about the right material to use, given our environmental and ethical constraints. We wanted it to be vegan (so no leather) but very durable (so no synthetic leather), and as eco-friendly and beautiful as possible. Finally, Gabriel drew from his Portuguese origins by choosing... cork! Yes, cork, a natural material emblematic of Portugal (which is the world's largest producer), is also non-slip, waterproof, durable, and light! We are really satisfied with the result.

 

New product - Milkweed scarf
Our technical developments have also allowed us to add a new product to our winter collection: a milkweed-insulated scarf, the first in history to be commercially produced. Warm, soft, and light, the Lasclay scarf brings us one step closer to our dream: keeping people warm with milkweed, from head to toe. We are currently finishing pictures of the product, which will be available for pre-orders in a few weeks.
