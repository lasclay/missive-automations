We just finished drying and processing tons of milkweed seed pods, carefully picked during what is probably the shortest and most challenging harvesting season of any crop.

Milkweed has an extremely short harvest window: only about 10 days before the mature pods open and the silky fibres fly away with the wind. If we miss that moment, the harvest literally disappears into the air. 

And since no existing farm machinery existed for this unique plant when we started our company, we had to invent our own harvesting equipment, build prototypes, and refine the process year after year during that short season. Hand-picking is possible, but extremely labour-intensive and far too slow to produce meaningful volumes at scale. It wouldn't do much for our goal to use perennial milkweed farming as a mechanism to save threatened monarch butterflies from extinction.

 

And every night after a long day in the fields, the challenge continues: Since milkweed seed pods are harvested at 70% moisture, they have to be immediately dried below 15%. If we don't do that, the pods' shells will rot, collapse unto themselves and ruin the precious fibers and seeds that we use to make our products.

 
 
See, milkweed fibers are naturally hollow (like polar bear hair), forming tiny tubes that trap air and create an exceptional thermal, acoustic, antibacterial and water-repellent barrier. Light yet highly insulating, they rival—or even surpass—synthetic and animal materials like Primaloft, goose down or merino wool, without the ecological or ethical cost. Coated in a natural wax, these fibers repel both rain and snow from the outside and moisture/sweat from the inside, keeping you warm and dry in all conditions, with thin clothes or accessories.

At every step of the growing, harvesting, drying and extracting processes, we must do our best to treat the fibers gently (while preserving productivity) to preserve the fibers' hollow structure and natural wax that make them warm and waterproof.
 
 
 
When intact, the milkweed hollow fibers are used as the main insulation component in more than 30 amazing products that we manufacture locally with partially automated equipment: 
 
  • Some made for winter, like our popular mittens, scarves, neck warmers, insoles and beanies that were tested and approved in the Arctic's freezing temperatures.

  • Some made for summer and lifestyle, like our lunch bags and can cozies that use milkweed's tremendous insulating power to keep food and drinks fresh.

  • We even have milkweed/monarchs-inspired spin-off products like skin care, blankets, jewelry and materials (milkweed batting to make your own products, and highly moisturizing milkweed seed oil to make homemade skin care).
 
Milkweed is a true, rare win-win for nature and humans.
