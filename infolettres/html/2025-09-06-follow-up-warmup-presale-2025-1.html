How is it going? Believe it or not, September is almost here already! I hope you’re still fully enjoying the warm season as it comes to a close and that, like us, you’re just as excited to welcome the crisp air of fall, with its dazzling colors and, quite sadly, shorter days.
 
For the iconic monarch butterflies that we made our company's mission to save from extinction, the arrival of fall is the trigger to begin their epic migration—traveling more than 4,000 miles south across the continent, all the way to the forests of northern Mexico and California.
 
All summer long, we watched them peacefully as they flew, laid eggs, hatched, fed, formed their chrysalises, and emerged once again by the thousands in our vast milkweed fields—now serving as an emergency refuge for them at a time when, everywhere else, milkweed, the only host plant and food of their caterpillar, is being destroyed through pesticides, municipal mowing, and urban expansion on once-wild farmland.
As a token of thanks for this lifeline, the monarchs offer us an invaluable gift each year: tons of seed pods, born of their precious pollination work, filled with seeds and silky fibers whose many properties had long been forgotten—until quite recently!
 
Milkweed fibers are naturally hollow (like polar bear hair), forming tiny tubes that trap air and create an exceptional thermal, acoustic, antibacterial and water-repellent barrier. Light yet highly insulating, they rival—or even surpass—synthetic and animal materials like Primaloft, goose down or merino wool, without the ecological or ethical cost. Coated in a natural wax, these fibers repel both rain and snow from the outside and moisture/sweat from the inside, keeping you warm and dry in all conditions, with thin clothes or accessories.
 
We use these hollow fibers as the main insulation component in more than 30 products that we manufacture locally with partially automated equipment: 
 
  • Some made for winter, like our popular mittens, scarves, neck warmers, insoles and beanies that were tested and approved in the Arctic's freezing temperatures.

  • Some made for summer and lifestyle, like our lunch bags and can cozies that use milkweed's tremendous insulating power to keep food and drinks fresh.
 
Milkweed is a true, rare win-win for nature and humans.
